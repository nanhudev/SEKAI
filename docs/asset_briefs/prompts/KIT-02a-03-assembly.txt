=== FOLLOW-UP: KIT-02a - ASSEMBLY DEMOS (D1 / D2 / D3) ===

The 20 pieces are built and I have looked at them. Now build three assemblies.

These are COMPOSITIONS, not layout proofs. Showing that modules tile is not the
requirement. The requirement is that each assembly produces a FEELING. An assembly
that only demonstrates "the modules fit together" is a FAIL.

Reuse the existing 20 pieces as objects. You may rotate them in 90 degree steps
around the vertical axis and place them at positions of your choosing. You may NOT
stretch them, and you may NOT change the modulus.

Everything goes in ONE collection named SEKAI_KIT02A_ASSEMBLY, and all three
assemblies go into one export file. Add the pieces as LINKED copies or as separate
mesh data - whichever keeps the file exportable - but each assembly must be
visually distinct in width, step count, riser and side treatment. Differing only
in length is a FAIL.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1 - GUILD TERRACE  (civic / proud / functional)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A town's civic stair. NOT a mountain path.

Build: market-edge plaza -> Stair_R16_7W4 -> Terrace_Landing_2x2 with a balustrade
-> Stair_R20_7W4 -> upper terrace -> forecourt, with a simple guild frontage
(a wall with a doorway opening is enough) as the terminating axis.

Use Alcove_2 as the mid-landing seat. Put End_2 where flights meet the terraces so
no flight ends in mid-air.

It must show: the WIDTH, the public space, and evidence that residents would really
walk this way - drainage, worn centre lines, a lamp or post footings, a place to
stand and talk.

ACCEPTANCE: photographed from the plaza, it must read as "I am climbing the steps
of an important public building" - not "I am climbing a hill path".

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
D2 - CLIFF ASCENT  (the highest-spec assembly of this pass)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A long climb in three distinct height stages. "Eight identical modules in a line"
is explicitly forbidden, and so is pure symmetry and one single composition
throughout.

Between the stages there must be: a landing - a retaining wall - rock interaction -
a rail where there is a real drop - ONE ancient fragment - ONE vista opening.

Suggested build:
    End_2 -> Stair_R24_7W2 x 4 -> Alcove_2 -> Stair_R24_7W2 x 3 ->
    Cantilever_Deck_2 -> Stair_R16_7W2 x 2 -> Stair_Broken_2 -> Terrace_Landing_2x2
Wrap the flank in Cheek_R_2 and Retain_Wall_Step_2, and put the ancient fragment
where the stair changes direction, so the turn is disguised by something old.

The route must TURN at least once. It must not be a straight run. The player should
lose sight of the top from the bottom.

ACCEPTANCE - the two shots that decide it:
  - from the bottom: the image must produce "I need to climb that"
  - from the top, looking back: the image must produce "I came up from there"
The whole ascent must read as PROGRESSION, not as a stamina test.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
D3 - HALF-BURIED OLD PATH
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

This one is about VISUAL INTEGRATION, not geometry.

Build from Rock_Stair_Half_2 x 4 with an End_2 and a Broken_2 in the middle.

The stairs must not stand NEXT TO a rock - they must GROW OUT OF THE HILL. Rock
overlaps the cheeks. Soil covers the outer nosings. Vegetation slots sit between
the flights. Corners are broken and rounded. Some treads have lost their shape
entirely and read only as a change in the rock.

ACCEPTANCE: the primary read must be "an old path that has been in this mountain
for a very long time" - the stones are the SUBSTRATE, not the subject.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
PLACEMENT RULES FOR ALL THREE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Build each assembly around its own local origin so the three do not overlap, and
  so I can export them as one file without them intersecting.
- Do not scale anything. rotation 0 or a whole 90 degree multiple, scale 1.
- Do not add new materials. Only the four ENV-01 materials.
- Do not add textures or node trees that GLB cannot export.
- Keep the assemblies inside the same triangle budget as the kit (under 26,000 for
  the whole scene).

OUTPUT
One fenced Python code block:

    # C2B:CHUNK assembly_demo

that creates all three assemblies. No prose. Then print, for each assembly, its
bounding box, its object count, its step count and its riser mix, so I can verify
that the three really do differ in width, step count, riser and side treatment.

Do not write an export chunk - I export with my own tooling.
