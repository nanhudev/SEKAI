WHAT IS WRONG

PRIMARY and SECONDARY are there and they read: the slope, the width, the stepped
cheek, the threshold stone, the nosing chamfers, and the material break-up between
base and pale stone all survive the render.

TERTIARY is missing. At first-person distance the treads read as flat, clean
planes with crisp straight edges - they look like newly cut blocks, not like a
public building's stairs that people have walked on for a generation. Every
detail cause I asked for either is not in the mesh or is too small to see at
1.5 m, and the result reads "clean block kit" rather than "built stone".

WHAT I WANT CHANGED - all of it on the VISUAL mesh only, no dimension, no count,
no material, no origin, no rotation/scale change:

1. THE CENTRE OF TRAFFIC IS WORN. On every tread the middle third should be
   slightly dished and its surface broken up, while both edges stay rough and
   flat. Right now every tread is a single flat plane edge to edge. This is the
   single most important change: a dished centre line is what makes a stair look
   walked on, and it is visible from 1.5 m.

2. THE OUTER NOSINGS ARE DAMAGED, THE INNER ONES ARE CRISP. Break the front edge
   of the upper-outer nosings: chipped, rounded, one corner missing outright on
   two or three steps. The inner nosings must stay sharp so the asymmetry reads
   as a cause and not as sloppiness. Do not damage both sides equally.

3. ONE DRAINAGE CUT. Cut a shallow channel through one nosing and let it run back
   down the tread, and make the two stones downstream of it read scoured - paler,
   smoother, no moss. One is enough; two would read as a pattern.

4. MOSS IN THE JOINTS, ON THE UPHILL SIDE. Moss must sit in the joint lines and
   in the nosing joints on the uphill half of the width, not as green panels on
   tread tops. In the render the moss currently reads as green rectangles laid on
   top of steps, which looks like a decal. It should look like something that
   grew in a gap because water sits there.

5. STONE COURSES MUST VARY MORE. You did vary them, but the variation is even -
   long-short-long-short. Make at least one course break with a genuinely short
   stone (about 0.4 m of the 4.00 m width) and one with a long one (about 1.6 m),
   so the joint rhythm cannot be predicted. A stair whose courses alternate on a
   regular beat reads as a texture, not as masonry.

6. THE CHEEK SHOULD READ AS COPING, NOT AS A RAMP. The flank wall's top follows
   the nosing line, which is right, but it should do so in visible course steps
   with a distinct cap course on top that overhangs very slightly, rather than as
   one smooth stepped mass.
